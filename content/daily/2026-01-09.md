Today we're going to read [[GDPO: Group reward-Decoupled Normalization  Policy Optimization for Multi-reward RL  Optimization]]. 

The end goal will be to frame protein sequence design as episodic RL — the actions mutate residues, rewards for predicted binding affinity (via ESMFold), stability (energy), and drug-likeness (QED/SAscore). Integrate GDPO with ProteinGym datasets; visualize sequence landscapes as 2D/3D embeddings (t-SNE/UMAP), fitness Pareto curves, and animated mutation paths highlighting trade-offs
- Proteins are long chains of building blocks called amino acids that fold into specific 3D shapes to function, like keys fitting locks in the body
	- designing new ones for biotech means tweaking their "letter sequence" (20 standard amino acids) to create custom shapes for drugs or enzymes
	- This project treats design as a game where an AI (using GDPO) iteratively improves sequences by changing one letter at a time, guided by scores for how well the protein binds targets, stays stable, and acts drug-like.
- The actions in this case is the following:
	- Residues are individual amino acids in the chain, like letters in a word. Mutating means swapping one letter (e.g., A to G) at a chosen spot per step, building better sequences over episodes (full design trials).
- The rewards:
	- ESMFold is an AI tool that reads a sequence and predicts its 3D shape; high affinity rewards strong shape matches to a target (e.g., disease protein), like better key-lock fit
	- Measures how "happy" the folded shape is using physics scores like Rosetta energy—lower (more negative) means tighter packing, less unfolding risk
	- QED scores overall "niceness" for pills (balance of size, bonds); SAscore estimates lab synthesis ease—higher QED, lower SA for ideal drugs
- We will use ProteinGym datasets — Huge collections of real lab data on millions of protein mutants (sequence tweaks) and their measured fitness from experiments like deep mutational scanning
	- Sequence landscapes as 2D/3D embeddings (t-SNE/UMAP): Maps similar sequences as nearby dots in a picture, colored by scores, to spot good regions like hills on a terrain.
	- Fitness Pareto curves: Lines plotting trade-offs (e.g., high binding but medium stability), showing GDPO finds balanced winners.
	- Animated mutation paths: Videos of sequences evolving step-by-step, highlighting improving scores and trade-offs

[[GRPO causes model collapse due to advantage summing]]. GDPO normalizes each [[Reward advantages provide us the training objective in policy gradient methods|Reward advantages]] first, then sum it, and then batch normalizes that sum. [[Batch normalization in GDPO ensures that every batch of questions is taken into account when normalizing]]. This is helpful to our project because:
- With GRPO, there's a high likelihood in advantage collapse with ESMFold, Rosetta Energy, and QED rewards
- If advantages collpase, there will be fewer explored trade-off points. But, [[GDPO provides more distinct advantage groups than GRPO]] which means richer Pareto frontier data

- Is RL + LLMs enough for AGI?
	- [[Reinforcement Learning with verifiable rewards enables LLMs to reach expert performance in task domains]]
		- Things to look into from [this paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23829v1)
			- RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) Generalizes Past Code/Math Domains
				- Traditional RLVR was strongest where answers are clearly and objectively verifiable—e.g., passing unit tests (code), correct numerical/theorem answer
				- RLVR can be extended to much broader, less-structured domains (medicine, chemistry, psychology, economics) by using model-based reward models (LLMs as verifiers), not just rigid rules.
			- Rule-based rewards (e.g., regex/exact match, test-pass) break down with long, free-form, or multi-step answers (only ~45% of real exam Q&A have easy one-word verification!)
				- Generative/model-based rewards ask a verifier LLM “does this response match the reference?” which can handle nuanced, less-structured answers and scales to more diverse tasks.
				- The paper shows that even a moderately-sized reward model (e.g., 7B, distilled from a large LLM) is effective across domains and much more scalable than rule-based evaluation.
			- RLVR typically used binary (pass/fail) signals. This is simple, but problematic for open-ended answers.
				- The authors introduce soft reward scoring: the verifier LLM outputs a probability that a final answer is correct (e.g., “0.77”), providing RL with a gradient rather than only 1/0.
				- Soft rewards proved especially robust in tasks/domains with ambiguous, fuzzy, or partial correctness (multi-subject college QA, etc.)—encourage RL agents to keep climbing even without perfect, crisp answers.
			- Robustness: RLVR Reduces Need for Large Manual Annotation
				- If you have an objective expert reference answer, you do not need massive annotation or training a reward model per domain: strong LLM verifiers agree very well (~86-88% agreement, Cohen’s kappa) across tasks (GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct).
				- Instead, you can distill verifier knowledge by bootstrapping: LLM verifies new responses during RL rollout, generating more cheap training data for the reward model.
			- Scaling and Out-of-Distribution Transfer
				- Model-based rewards scale better with bigger data and more tasks than rule-based.
				- Binary rule-based rewards even degrade with scale; model-based continue to improve.
				- Their experiments: model-based RL outperforms both SFT (supervised finetune) and rule-based RL on OOD benchmarks (NaturalReasoning, WebInstruct).
			- Concepts to Look Into
				- Reward model distillation (automatically training small reward models from bigger LLM “teacher” verifiers)
				- Analysis of reward signal stability across RL stages/domains (why rule-based rewards degrade with scale)
				- Generative vs. discriminative models for rewards in RL
				- Out-of-distribution generalization of reward models
				- The role of reference-free reward modeling for tasks without any reference answers, and how contextual LLMs might “judge” such outputs
	- [Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards Implicitly Incentivizes Correct Reasoning in Base LLMs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14245)
		- CoT-Pass@K Metric and the Shortcomings of Pass@K
			- Pass@K, a standard metric, is insufficient for measuring improvement in genuine reasoning, because base LLMs often get the right answer by luck or spurious chains, not valid reasoning paths.
			- The authors introduce CoT-Pass@K, which credits only answers where both the final answer and its entire chain-of-thought (CoT) are correct.
			- Implication: RL with verifiable rewards doesn't just bias the sampling toward lucky guesses; it genuinely improves the logical integrity of the LLM's reasoning steps, as measured by CoT-Pass@K.
		- Theoretical Foundation: Why RLVR Incentivizes Correct Reasoning
			- Standard RL can be "goal-getting" without caring about the steps; but RLVR, when using deterministic verifiers and LLM chains of thought, implicitly aligns policy gradients toward logically sound reasoning.
			- The paper provides a theorem showing RLVR increases the probability of generating correct chains of thought and not just correct answers, under mild assumptions (because correct CoTs are much likelier to yield verifiable correct answers).
			- Implication: The optimization learns to build explanations (not just outcomes), which is different from RL in environments with less structure
		- CoT-Judging: Automated Chain-of-Thought Verification
			- Scaling RLVR to real problems requires mass evaluation of reasoning chains. The paper uses a LLM-as-CoT-judge paradigm—running several independent verifications per chain and using "any-correct," "all-correct," or "majority-correct" counting to robustly label reasoning chains as correct or not.
			- This methodology addresses the practical bottleneck of reasoning evaluation at scale. Future RLVR work likely needs more robust, possibly cross-model or human-audited CoT verifiers.
		- RLVR Training Dynamics
			- The paper analyzes training logs and finds the improvement in reasoning chains emerges early and generalizes smoothly during training—not just at the endpoint.
			- There is an important distinction between easy and hard examples: for easy ones, Pass@1 goes to 1.0 quickly, but CoT correctness lags much more, highlighting the importance of direct reasoning supervision.
		- Overthinking, Long CoTs, and Failure Modes
			- RLVR sometimes incentivizes longer chains of thought because longer CoTs are more likely to contain enough information to solve harder problems. But this can sometimes lead to "overthinking" (i.e., overly verbose or redundant reasoning).
			- RLVR may reinforce spurious or logically-inconsistent CoTs if the prior (base model) is not well-prepared; so pre-training and example selection are important.
		- Concepts to look into
			- Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO, the algorithm used for RLVR)—how it works and why it’s suited for verifiable reward setups.
			- Scaling laws for RLVR and the “bitter lesson”: What it would take for RLVR to be as transformative as scaling pre-training.

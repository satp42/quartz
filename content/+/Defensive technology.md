Offensive technologies enable actors to threaten or harm others — military weapons, viruses, surveillance tools that enable oppression. Defensive technologies focus on preservation and protection: vaccines, cryptography, fortifications, air filtration. The distinction matters because the balance between them decides whether society is defense-favoring from coercive and centralized control. This is why [[Defensive technologies let societies manage threats through decentralized governance]], hence why [[Defensive technologies should be emphasized in technological growth]].

# Major Examples

**Kinetic & Physical Defense**
- Systems include air and missile defense (e.g., interceptors, radar networks), armored vehicles, hardened bases, and passive protection like bunkers and blast walls
	- Autonomous and semi‑autonomous systems—drones, ground robots, logistics vehicles—reduce risk to humans and can provide swarming or perimeter defense
- Soldier protection features helmets, body armor, wearable sensors, helmet‑mounted radar, and self‑healing armor materials that improve survivability while reducing weight
	- Self‑healing composites, lighter ballistic materials, and improved thermal/EM shielding enhance protective gear and platforms
	- Additive manufacturing enables rapid production of spare parts and customized protective components near the front, improving sustainment
	- Defensive bio technologies include pathogen surveillance, secure laboratories, vaccines, antivirals, and protocols to prevent misuse of biological agents
	- Governance covers access control to dangerous strains, screening of DNA synthesis orders, and monitoring of emerging biotechnologies like genome editing
**Cyber defense**
- Defensive cybersecurity protects networks and data through practices like firewalls, intrusion detection/prevention systems, zero‑trust architectures, and encryption
	- Cyberbiosecurity studies vulnerabilities at the intersection of biological systems, digital infrastructure, and cyber‑physical devices
	- Threats include tampering with lab automation, genomic data breaches, and manipulation of bio‑manufacturing processes, with corresponding defensive controls across IT, OT, and lab systems
- Modern stacks integrate AI‑driven threat detection, prescriptive security that blocks attacks pre‑impact, and automated incident response for resilience — [[The instability of human-AI power equilibriums]]
**Intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance**
- Radar, electro‑optical/infrared sensors, sonar, and space‑based systems provide early warning and targeting information.
	- AI supports decision‑making by fusing sensor data, predicting threats, and recommending courses of action in real time
- Uncrewed systems (drones, UGVs, USVs) extend ISR reach, operating in contested areas for persistent surveillance and target tracking
**Space and communications security**
- Space systems for defense include satellites for secure communications, navigation, and missile warning, plus anti‑jam and hardening measures
	- Photonic systems promise high‑bandwidth, low‑EMI processing for future sensors and signal processing in defense applications
- Secure communications use spread‑spectrum, frequency hopping, end‑to‑end encryption, and increasingly post‑quantum cryptography to resist interception and decryption
	- Quantum computing and quantum‑resistant crypto shape both offensive code‑breaking and defensive secure communication strategies

The problem with current solutions to [[In-situ resource extraction is going to be pivotal for deep-space exploration|ISRU extraction]] rovers is that they are utilizing existing exploration rovers and attaching an end excavation arm. However, this utilizes a singular rover model which limits the carrying capacity of how much [[Lunar Regolith]] can be extracted. However, a modular rover system like the one in the [[Galactic Resource Utilization Space Inc. External Whitepaper]] allows for multiple [[GRU labs will have transport robot to move regolith from the miner to base|Transport robots]] to live within the chassis of [[GRU labs will include an excavator frame to house transport robots and mine regolith|Excavator frames]] so that once the carrying capacity of a transport robot is reached, it can go back to base and have another transport robot come into the excavator frame.

Additionally, the Excavator frame houses the power units for generation and distribution where the transport robots can come back and instantly get charged. This is better than direct charging because the hub provides fully-charged lithium ion batteries while the transport rovers complete tasks, which allows for instantaneous power module exchange.

In terms of the standardized battery modules, various papers have tried to house 6S-LiPo batteries with swapping mechanisms, [[Two-way heat pumps can be used for thermal control on rovers]], and battery management system to handle battery swaps

This idea, called the **normalized compression distance (NCD)**, allows us to compare objects. For instance, we can measure the similarity between two books by seeing how well they compress when concatenated, compared to compressing them separately. It has been used for everything from creating phylogenetic trees in biology to plagiarism detection.

Similar to cosine similarity maybe.

These practical measures come with a caveat. They depend on the specific compressor used. A file that compresses well with one algorithm might not with another. However, for many types of data, different good compressors tend to agree on what is complex and what is simple, making them powerful tools for exploring the structure of real-world data.

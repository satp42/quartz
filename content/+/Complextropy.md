It aims to quantify the [[Kolmogorov complexity]] of a physical state by finding the simplest efficient model that describes its structure, while treating the rest as random noise.

The complextropy of an n-bit physical state $x$ is the length of the shortest, most efficient program that can generate a family of similar states, where $x$ is a typical, unexceptional member of that family.

Formally, let's say a program $P$ samples from a distribution $D$ over a set of states $S$. The complextropy of a state $x$ is the minimum size of an efficient program $P$ such that $x$ is efficiently incompressible given samples from $D$.

**Complextropy follows a distinct arc: it starts low for simple states, rises as structure emerges, and falls again as the system approaches random equilibrium.**

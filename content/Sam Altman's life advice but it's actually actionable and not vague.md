This is based on Sam's [article](https://blog.samaltman.com/the-days-are-long-but-the-decades-are-short). Not a diss to it, it's a really great article that you should definitely check out. The act of me writing this is more of an exercise to plan on how I can incorporate it into my life.
1. "Never put your family, friends, or significant other low on your priority list.  Prefer a handful of truly close friends to a hundred acquaintances.  Don’t lose touch with old friends.  Occasionally stay up until the sun rises talking to people.  Have parties."
	1. Make a list of priorities that you reference when [journaling about decisions](https://fs.blog/decision-journal/) you're about to make
	2. Find 10 people you find closest to you aside from family. Judge who these friends are on an axis of how you pleasurable they are to hang out with, and how beneficial they are (i.e. how much you learn, how much they motivate you, how much they influence you positively, etc.). Focus on having that list of 10 be in the first quadrant. Make a CRM on [Clay](https://clay.earth/) and get reminded to send a text and have a conversation with them everyday. Review this list/CRM every month
	3. Go through your messages and calendars and find close friends that are on the top 10 list (or near the fringe of it) that you haven't talked to in the last 3 months — reach out to them and set a calendar event to do something with them.
	4. Throw parties and talk to people till the sun rises.
2. "Life is not a dress rehearsal—this is probably it.  Make it count.  Time is extremely limited and goes by fast.  Do what makes you happy and fulfilled—few people get remembered hundreds of years after they die anyway.  Don’t do stuff that doesn’t make you happy (this happens most often when other people want you to do something).  Don’t spend time trying to maintain relationships with people you don’t like, and cut negative people out of your life.  Negativity is really bad.  Don’t let yourself make excuses for not doing the things you want to do."
	1. Every morning answer the following questions
		1. "How excited am I to go do what I'm about to do today?"
			- If you are excited, then good, keep doing what you're doing
			- If you are not excited, then ask yourself what is exciting you and what is keeping you from doing it
		2. "To what extent would I have been happy if I was told that I have to wake up an hour earlier to do the thing I am about to do today?"
			- If you are still happy, then good, keep doing what you're doing
			- If you are not happy, then ask yourself what is something that is constantly occupying your mind
		3. "If I told you today was the last day of your life and you had to do what you were planning to do, would you regret it?"
			- If you are still happy, then good, keep doing what you're doing
			- If you are not happy, figure out why you would be regretful. Often times it's because your current plan keeps you from doing something else. Your job is then to figure out what that something else is and find out how to do it by any means necessary.
		4. "To what extent am I doing this because I want to do it, or because someone else told me to do it?"
			- If you are doing this for yourself and your happy, then good, keep doing what you're doing
			- If you are doing this for yourself and your not happy, then figure out what issues with the way you do it is causing that. Often times, you are doing your life's mission, but something about how you do it is causing that issue. It may be the people you are doing it, where your doing it, the amount of money you make doing it, etc.
			- If you are doing it for someone else, figure out what you would do for yourself regardless of money. Even if you're happy doing something that someone else told you, you will be a lot more happier doing something on your own terms.
	2. Go through your calendar and audit what each event that led to energy boosting and energy drainers. Remove all energy draining activities from your calendar in the future
	3. Figure out your **ikigai** — what it is that you are really good at, what is it that you truly believe the world needs, what is it that you are passionate about, and what will make you money? Then find the intersection of all four and do that for the rest of your life.
3. "How to succeed: pick the right thing to do (this is critical and usually ignored), focus, believe in yourself (especially when others tell you it’s not going to work), develop personal connections with people that will help you, learn to identify talented people, and work hard.  It’s hard to identify what to work on because original thought is hard."
	1. Find your ikigai.
		1. Write down 20 of your goals, isolate the top 3 goals that are related to your ikigai, and forget the other 17 goals.
		2. Write the steps it takes to reach goals — mini OKRs for your life
		3. Plan out your year to reach each OKR, then plan your quarter based on meeting each key result, then plan your week to make progress on the key result, and then plan your day to meet that weekly plan
		4. Do deep work everyday on a singular thing for 75% of your day — shouldn't be a problem because this should be something you're excited to wake up everyday and work on
	2. Make a list of the top 20 people every quarter that will help you reach your objectives and then cold email/DM each person
		1. Every week to strive to meet with at least 5 of them. If you can do everyday, that's even better
		2. Make a list of qualities (even ask them what their qualities are) that make them who they are
		3. Spend hours online and talking to people that resemble the intersection of all these qualities
